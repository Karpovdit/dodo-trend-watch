We're at an inflection point in how buildings operate.

For decades, the model has been the same: sensors generate data, dashboards display it, humans interpret it, and someone decides what to do. Every step requires attention. Every step takes time.

AI agents break that chain.

Not by removing humans — but by giving buildings the ability to sense, reason, and act on their own, within guardrails we define. Peak shaving that happens automatically. Maintenance tickets that arrive pre-analyzed. HVAC inefficiencies caught before they become costly.

The key insight: individual agents solve individual problems. But when you orchestrate them into coordinated systems — task runners screening data, experts interpreting anomalies, building agents balancing comfort and cost — you get something new. An operational layer that owns outcomes, not just tasks.

Start with agents. Build toward agentic.

We wrote a comprehensive guide covering the full journey — from defining your first agent's scope to architecting multi-agent systems across a portfolio.
