The real question this article answers is systemic:

"Which protocol changes are ready to deploy right now, and which exist only on paper - and how does an IoT buyer avoid purchasing marketing instead of a working feature?"

This is a question about the gap between specification and reality. The article returns to one idea again and again: support in the spec ≠ support in the chip ≠ support in the stack ≠ working in the field. That is its real topic.

Practical takeaway: right now, while the market is not yet saturated, it makes sense to build a concrete vendor checklist (the article outlines one). Those who do this now will have working systems in a year - while competitors are figuring out why their "Bluetooth 6.3 device" cannot see the neighboring "Bluetooth 6.3 device" via IPT.
